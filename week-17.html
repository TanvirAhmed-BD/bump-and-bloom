Pregnancy Week 17: Your Baby's Skeleton Is Hardening

You're deep into the second trimester now, and big construction is underway: your pear-sized baby is in the middle of one of development's great material upgrades — a skeleton that started as soft, bendy cartilage is steadily turning into real bone. And this week we handle the question almost every mom asks around now — how much weight should I actually be gaining? — calmly, honestly, and without a shred of guilt. Here's your complete guide to week 17.

Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of a pear — roughly thirteen centimeters, about five inches from head to bottom, and weighing around one hundred and forty grams. The growth is steady and serious now — your baby will roughly double in weight over the next few weeks. This week's headline is the skeleton.

Until now, your baby's frame has been made of flexible cartilage — think of it as scaffolding. Now, week by week, that cartilage is hardening into bone, in a process called ossification. It begins in the long bones and will continue not just through pregnancy but for years — which is why babies are famously bendy, and why some soft spots remain in the skull even at birth, entirely by design.

The bones of the inner ear are hardening too, preparing the hearing system, and the skeleton is now firm enough to show up clearly on ultrasound. Also new: your baby is developing sweat glands across the skin, the umbilical cord is growing thicker and stronger, and the very first deposits of baby fat are beginning. Movements are getting more coordinated and more vigorous — rolling, kicking, and punching practice is underway — and the senses keep wiring up: the eyes perceive light, the taste buds are working, and your baby swallows and hiccups right on schedule.

Your body & symptoms: the bump has arrived

By seventeen weeks, most women have a bump that's clearly a bump — your uterus is now a couple of finger-widths below your belly button and rising. Your center of gravity is shifting, and your posture compensates: a sway in the lower back, a slightly wider stance. That's the root of the backache that often begins around now.

Supportive shoes, sitting with your feet up when you can, and gentle movement are the everyday answers. Your appetite is likely in its enthusiastic phase. Other common week-seventeen reports: an itchy belly as the skin stretches (moisturizer and loose waistbands are your friends); occasional dizziness, especially standing up fast or getting too warm, as your expanded blood volume and relaxed blood vessels negotiate with gravity — rise slowly, hydrate, and don't skip meals; increased vaginal discharge, where clear or milky is typical; and dreams — vivid, cinematic, sometimes bizarre pregnancy dreams are extremely common.

You're not prophetic; you're pregnant. And if you're feeling genuinely good this week, that's the second trimester delivering on its reputation.

The weight-gain question, answered calmly

Every body enters pregnancy differently, so there is no single number that fits everyone. What widely used guidelines generally describe is a total gain across the whole pregnancy that depends on where you started: for someone who began at what charts call an average weight, the commonly cited total is somewhere around twenty-five to thirty-five pounds. Those who started underweight are generally guided toward a bit more; those who started at a higher weight, a bit less.

Your doctor knows your chart, your history, and your baby — their guidance for you personally always outranks any article or video, including this one. As for pace: many women gain little in the first trimester — nausea sees to that — and then gain steadily through the middle months, often around a pound a week in the second trimester for those in the middle range. But real bodies don't do straight lines; you'll see flat weeks and sudden jumps.

What your doctor watches is the overall trend at your appointments, not any single weigh-in — try to borrow that calm. Two firm rules: pregnancy is not a time for dieting or trying to lose weight, ever; and it is also not a free-for-all you need to feel guilty about enjoying.

The sweet spot is boring and kind: regular meals, protein and fiber and color, snacks when you're hungry, and movement most days. The scale is one data point among many — let it be minor paperwork, not a verdict.

Looking ahead: the anatomy scan

One appointment now deserves a spot on your mental calendar in bold: the anatomy scan, usually done between about eighteen and twenty-two weeks — for many women, right around week twenty, which makes it possibly just three weeks away for you. This is the detailed ultrasound where the sonographer takes a grand tour: measuring the head, heart, spine, stomach, kidneys, and limbs, checking the placenta's position and the fluid around your baby, and confirming growth is on track. If it isn't booked yet, call this week — those slots genuinely fill.

Ask whether a partner or support person can come along, and whether the office wants you to arrive with a full bladder or an empty one; instructions vary, and there's nothing worse than guessing wrong in that particular department. Many families also find out the baby's sex at this scan, if they want to and the baby cooperates. Most appointments run twenty to forty-five minutes, and you usually leave with measurements, a report for your doctor, and — the part everyone frames — photos.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially heavy or with cramping · severe or one-sided pelvic pain that doesn't ease with rest · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine · fainting (an occasional wave of lightheadedness when you stand too fast is common, but actually fainting always earns a call) · fluid leaking from the vagina. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 17

  • Book the anatomy scan if you haven't — it's done between weeks 18–22, slots fill up, and it's worth asking about a support person and bladder instructions
  • Eat the sweet spot: regular meals with protein, fiber and color, snacks when you're hungry — and never diet in pregnancy
  • Rise slowly, hydrate, and don't skip meals to keep dizziness away
  • Supportive shoes, feet up when you can, and gentle movement for the new backache
  • Moisturizer and loose waistbands for the itchy, stretching belly

Key takeaways

  • Your baby's skeleton is hardening from cartilage into bone — ossification has begun
  • Healthy weight gain depends on your starting point; your doctor watches the trend, not single weigh-ins
  • Pregnancy is never a time for dieting — regular meals, color, and movement are the sweet spot
  • The anatomy scan (weeks 18–22) could be about three weeks away — book it if you haven't
