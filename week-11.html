Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of a lime, and the head still makes up about half of the total length — the brain remains the flagship project. The skill of the week is in the hands: fingers open and close into tiny practicing fists, nail beds are in place, tooth buds keep developing, hair follicles appear, and the ears glide into their final positions.

Inside, the pancreas has begun producing insulin, bones are hardening steadily, and the diaphragm — the breathing muscle — is under construction, setting the stage for hiccups later. And your baby is busy: stretching, flexing and somersaulting in the roomy amniotic sac. You can't feel any of it yet, but on an ultrasound the bouncing can be genuinely startling.

Your body & symptoms

For many women, nausea and the worst fatigue begin easing from around now, as hormone levels level off and the placenta carries more of the load — appetite often returns with them. "For many" is not "for all": if your nausea is still going strong, you haven't missed a deadline.

Your uterus is now about grapefruit-sized and rising out of your pelvis, which will soon relieve the pressure on your bladder. New arrivals can include headaches, brief dizziness if you stand too fast, the first hint of a linea nigra, and nails growing faster or feeling more brittle. Emotionally, anticipation mixed with impatience — the almost-there feeling — is a standard week-11 mood.

The NT scan: your 11-to-13-week window

NT stands for nuchal translucency — a measurement of the small pocket of fluid at the back of your baby's neck, taken by ultrasound between about week 11 and week 13 (plus a few days). Combined with blood tests, it forms the first-trimester screening estimate for certain chromosomal conditions.

Because the window is specific, book it as soon as your doctor suggests. And remember: it's a screening — a risk estimate, not a diagnosis.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Heavy bleeding · severe pain · fainting · fever · being unable to keep fluids down. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 11

  • Book the NT scan — it must happen in the week 11–13 window, so don't let it drift
  • Stand up in stages: brief dizziness is common if you rise too fast
  • Welcome your appetite back with regular, nourishing meals
  • Rest when the fatigue visits — the worst of it usually starts easing from here

Key takeaways

  • Your lime-sized baby can open and close its fingers; the diaphragm is forming
  • Nausea often begins to ease from now — but a week or two later is normal too
  • The NT scan must happen in the week 11–13 window — book it promptly
  • Screening gives a risk estimate, not a diagnosis
