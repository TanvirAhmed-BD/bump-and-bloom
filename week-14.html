Pregnancy Week 14: Your Baby Can Make Faces

The second trimester is officially underway, and your baby is putting on a show: this week your peach-sized little one is practicing facial expressions — tiny frowns, squints and grimaces, rehearsed in the dark. Meanwhile your appetite is marching back, and a new zingy twinge low in your belly has a name (and it's a reassuring one). Here's your complete guide to week 14.

Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of a peach — roughly eight and a half to nine centimeters, about three and a half inches from head to bottom, and weighing around forty-three grams. The body is now growing faster than the head: the neck has lengthened, lifting the head into a more upright, baby-like posture, and the arms have reached close to their final proportions.

The star development is the face — the facial muscles are getting their first workouts, practicing frowning, squinting and grimacing. That's pure muscle rehearsal, not feelings. The roof of the mouth has finished forming, your baby swallows amniotic fluid regularly and makes small practice breathing movements, the liver is producing bile, and the spleen is joining in blood-cell production alongside the kidneys.

Fine, soft hair called lanugo is beginning to sprout across the body. And on the question relatives are already asking: your baby's sex organs have developed, and on a very clear ultrasound an experienced eye can sometimes make a good guess around now — but "sometimes" and "guess" are the important words. The anatomy scan at around twenty weeks remains the far more reliable moment.

Your body & symptoms: appetite returns

Week fourteen is when many women turn the corner into genuinely feeling good. The placenta is fully running the hormone show, energy is up, and the famous appetite often returns with enthusiasm. Aim for nourishing and regular rather than perfect — and remember you're still not literally eating for two: in the second trimester, most guidelines suggest only a few hundred extra calories a day, like a good snack, not a second dinner.

Your uterus is rising well into your abdomen, and a genuine little bump is appearing for many women, particularly by evening. You may notice your hair behaving beautifully — pregnancy hormones slow normal shedding, so it often feels thicker (what isn't shed now tends to shed after birth instead, which is also normal). A little itchiness as skin stretches and possibly the first faint stretch marks may start around now — genetics plays the biggest role in stretch marks, and moisturizer is for comfort, not destiny. One quieter symptom that surprises people: a stuffy nose can begin around now, as increased blood flow swells the membranes.

Those sharp little twinges: round ligament pain

Let's name that twinge, because it startles almost everyone the first time. Two ligaments — strong, cord-like bands — run from the top of your uterus down toward the groin, holding it in place like guy-ropes on a tent. As your uterus grows, those ropes stretch, and when you move suddenly — standing up quickly, coughing, sneezing, laughing hard, rolling over in bed — a ligament can spasm, producing a brief, sharp or pulling ache low in your belly, often on the right side, because that ligament is typically the longer one.

The reassuring profile: it lasts seconds, or a few minutes at most; it's clearly triggered by a movement; and it eases when you rest, change position, or draw your knees up slightly. Warmth helps many women. Prevention is mostly about pace: move a little more slowly than your pre-pregnancy self, rise in stages, and flex your hips slightly before you cough or sneeze.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially if heavy or with cramping · severe pain, or pain that is constant and doesn't ease when you rest — remember, round ligament twinges are brief and positional, so anything stronger or stubborn is different · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine or dizziness · fainting · fluid leaking from the vagina. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 14

  • Chase the anatomy scan booking if it isn't done — the weeks 18–22 window fills fast
  • Ask your doctor about second-trimester screening if you want it — often offered around weeks 15–20, and entirely optional
  • Use your returning appetite well: protein, iron-rich foods, and color on the plate
  • Rebuild your movement routine if it lapsed — on most days, a good walk counts beautifully
  • Start a simple bump photo habit if you like — same spot, same angle, every week or two

Key takeaways

  • Your baby is practicing facial expressions — frowns, squints and grimaces
  • Round ligament pain is brief, positional, and eases with rest — know its name
  • Your appetite is back: think an extra nourishing snack, not a second dinner
  • Anatomy scan window is weeks 18–22 — book it if you haven't
