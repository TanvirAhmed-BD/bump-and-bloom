Pregnancy Week 39: Full Term!

Welcome to week 39, mama — FULL TERM. Two words, one enormous journey. Your baby, now about the size of a small watermelon, is officially, completely cooked — and yet their brain is developing faster right now than at almost any point in the entire pregnancy, which is exactly why these final days of waiting, however endless they feel, are still doing quiet, important work. This week is about the last stretch of the marathon: your baby's finishing school, the emotional challenge of the final wait, what your checkups are watching so closely now, and how to stay as comfortable and sane as possible while every phone buzz makes your heart jump. Here's your complete guide to week 39.

Your baby this week

Your baby now weighs about seven and a quarter pounds and measures around twenty inches — about the size of a small watermelon. Full term means exactly what it promises: every major system is built, matured, and ready for life on the outside. When your baby arrives from this week onward, they arrive as a finished newborn — no asterisks, no "for a baby born at…" footnotes.

That is the milestone this entire series has been walking toward, and it's yours. And yet — here's the beautiful secret of week 39 — your baby is still using every day. The brain is the headline: it is growing and wiring at a remarkable rate right now, building the connections that will handle everything from first cries to first words, and it will keep that pace for years after birth.

Layers of fat continue to smooth and insulate. The immune system keeps receiving antibodies from you — borrowed protection, delivered daily, that will stand guard over your baby's first weeks in a world full of new germs. Even the lungs, mature as they are, keep a final production line running.

So when the days feel like nothing but waiting, remember: your baby is not idle in there. They are doing the last, finest work of becoming — and the calendar in your body knows more about the schedule than any app does. Your baby also arrives pre-introduced to the soundtrack of home.

By now, research consistently suggests, newborns recognize the voices they've heard most through the womb's walls — yours above all — and even show a preference for the rhythms of the language spoken around them. That means the first conversation you have with your baby is, in a real sense, a continuation: you will be a familiar voice in a brand-new world, and there is no better comfort a newborn can be handed. Space, meanwhile, is genuinely minimal now: your baby is curled in the classic fetal position, head most likely down and low, movements all powerful rolls, pushes, and whole-body shifts.

The pattern, though — say it with me — stays the same. The same active hours, the same wakeful sessions, right up to birth. Your awareness of that rhythm remains one of the most valuable things you own this week.

Your baby's remaining job is choosing the day. Yours, for now, is the subject of the rest of this post: the art — and it is an art — of the final wait.

Your body & mind: the hardest waiting

Because let's be honest with each other, mama: for many women, week 39 is the hardest week of pregnancy — not physically, though it has its moments, but mentally. Physically, the picture is familiar by now: a heavy, low, fully-occupied pelvis; back and hip aches with tenure; Braxton Hicks and prodromal contractions that may run a convincing show every evening and fold by morning; sleep in fragments; and a body that feels, in the best description we've heard, like a house where the guest of honor is due at any moment but hasn't texted. All of it is normal.

All of it is miserable company at times. Both things are true. The mental game, though, is the real opponent.

By week 39, most women have a part-time job answering the question "any news?" — from family, friends, coworkers, the mail carrier. The kindness underneath it is real, and so is the pressure it builds: as if the baby's arrival were a deadline you were personally missing. You are not missing anything.

Your baby is not late; your baby is not even due yet — and when the due date itself passes next week, we'll talk about how ordinary that is, too. For now, some protective measures, offered with love: deploy that copy-paste reply from week 38 without a shred of guilt. Consider going quiet online for a few days.

Let your partner or a friend be the public-relations department. And curate your input fiercely — this is a beautiful week to mute dramatic birth stories, dramatic headlines, and dramatic anyone, and spend your attention on things that make you laugh, rest, and feel like yourself. You have spent nine months becoming a mother. This week, you are allowed to simply be a person waiting — warm, fed, and off duty from the world's curiosity.

What your checkups are watching now

Your weekly checkups continue in week 39, and while they follow the familiar route, it's worth understanding what your team is actually watching — because this close to the finish, the quiet routine carries real weight. Blood pressure and urine at every visit: still screening for preeclampsia, which can appear even in the final days, and which your warning-sign knowledge now covers so well. Your belly's growth and the baby's heartbeat: checking that your baby continues to grow and thrive on schedule.

Your baby's position and how low they've settled: felt by your doctor's practiced hands. And your own report — sleep, swelling, headaches, movement, contractions — which is why your observations matter; you are a member of this team, and your data is the kind they can't get from any machine. Two conversations become common around now.

First, some doctors offer to check your cervix at these visits — a brief internal exam to see whether it has begun softening, thinning, or opening. Whether to have one is a genuine choice, and it's the kind of thing worth discussing openly: what it can tell you, what it can't predict — a cervix can sit unchanged for weeks or change overnight — and how you'll feel about the answer either way. Ask, decide together, and hold the result loosely whatever it is.

Second, the "what happens next week" conversation begins: if your baby hasn't arrived by your due date, what does your practice do — how often will they see you, what extra monitoring might they suggest, and at what point would they talk with you about options for encouraging labor? There are no decisions to make today; the point of asking now is simply that next week, nothing your doctor raises will be a surprise. Knowledge is the week's other comfort measure.

Bring your final birth plan copy to one of these visits, too, if you haven't filed it in your chart yet — our Birth Plan post in this series can still help you finish the one calm page — and run one last confirmation of the practical script with your team: whom you call, where you go, and what they want you to do at each stage, day or night. Hearing it once more, out loud, in week 39 has a way of settling the nerves like nothing else on the checklist.

Comfort in the final days

Now, the comfort briefing — week 39 edition, field-tested by generations of women in exactly your shoes. Rest remains the headliner: nap when sleep offers itself, in whatever position your pillow village allows, and treat anyone who calls rest "lazy" this week as a person whose opinion has expired. Gentle movement balances it: a slow walk around the block often does more for mood, swelling, and sleep than anything else on this list — and if the walk turns into a waddle-past-the-neighbors performance, embrace it; you are a parade of one.

Warmth is your other old friend: a warm shower aimed at an aching back or shoulders is a small, daily mercy, and your support person can learn exactly where the ache lives and apply counter-pressure — good practice for labor, by the way. Keep the practicals at parade rest: bag by the door, car seat in, phone charged, tank full if there's a car involved, snacks that travel well, and the phone list from week 36 somewhere your partner can find it half-asleep. And then — the secret weapon of the final days — plan small pleasures that don't depend on the baby's timing: the good takeout, the film you've been saving, the friend who makes you laugh, the long phone call with someone who will not once ask "any news?" A life that still contains nice things is the best waiting room ever designed.

You are not putting life on hold, mama. You are living the last days of this chapter — and they count.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Regular contractions that are strengthening and settling into a true pattern lead the everyday list now: time them, and call your doctor or birthplace following the guidance your team has given you — remembering week 38’s golden rule that their instructions outrank every general pattern, including ours. Your water breaking — a gush, or a trickle you can’t stop — is a call right away, contractions or not, and especially if the fluid looks green or brown or has a strong smell. Also call right away for: a clear decrease in your baby’s movements, or a pattern that feels different from their normal — at full term, in the tightest quarters of all, the pattern is still the pattern, and it stays law to the very last day · bleeding that is bright red, flowing, or like a period — distinct from the small amount of blood-tinged mucus of a show · the preeclampsia signs: a severe headache that doesn’t ease with rest · vision changes such as spots, blurring, or flashing lights · sudden swelling of your face or hands · pain high in your belly, especially under your right ribs · sudden, rapid weight gain · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. And call emergency services for severe breathlessness or chest pain — never wait-and-see symptoms, in pregnancy or out of it. Most moms meet none of this; every mom should know it. And in week 39 there’s a silver lining to the phone call: this close to the finish, the person who answers may be about to help you write the best day of your life so far.
