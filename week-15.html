Pregnancy Week 15: Your Baby Can Sense Light

This week, your apple-sized baby is doing something quietly astonishing: sensing light. The eyes are still fused shut, but the developing retinas already respond to brightness filtering through your belly. Add the beginnings of hiccups and forming taste buds, and week 15 is a busy one in there. Your own headline is likely far less poetic — a permanently stuffy nose. Here's your complete guide to week 15.

Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of an apple — roughly ten centimeters, about four inches from head to bottom, and weighing around seventy grams. The legs have now grown longer than the arms, all the joints move, and your baby is busy practicing breathing movements, swallowing amniotic fluid, and sucking — some babies have already found their thumb.

The sense of the week is light: your baby's eyes remain sealed shut — they won't open for many weeks yet — but the retinas are already responding to light that filters through your abdominal wall, and babies have been seen on ultrasound shifting in response to a very bright light outside. Taste is joining in too: taste buds are forming on the tongue, the beginning of a system that will soon let your baby sample the flavors of your meals through the amniotic fluid.

Somewhere around now, your baby may also begin to hiccup — the diaphragm practicing, in effect. You almost certainly can't feel them yet; later in pregnancy they may arrive as gentle, rhythmic little taps. The ears are moving toward their final position, the bones keep hardening and are now visible on ultrasound, and fine lanugo hair is spreading across the body.

Your body & symptoms: hello, stuffy nose

The likely star of your week fifteen is your nose. A stuffy, congested nose — sometimes with the odd nosebleed — is one of pregnancy's most common and least glamorous symptoms; it even has a nickname: "pregnancy rhinitis." The cause is plumbing: your blood volume has climbed significantly, and that extra blood flow swells the delicate membranes inside your nose — and your gums, which may bleed a little when you brush; also common, also hormonal.

Saline spray or rinse, a humidifier by the bed, staying hydrated, and propping your head up slightly at night help many women. One caution: many over-the-counter decongestants aren't the default choice in pregnancy — check with your doctor or pharmacist before using medication, even the familiar stuff. Elsewhere, your bump is becoming a real presence, heartburn may be starting its slow climb — smaller, more frequent meals and not lying flat right after eating are the classic countermeasures — and the skin across your belly may stretch and itch: moisturizer there is comfort, not vanity.

Sleeping positions: practice season

Later in pregnancy, many doctors recommend side-sleeping — especially as the uterus gets heavy — because lying flat on your back for long stretches can, for some women, compress a major blood vessel and reduce blood flow, leaving you dizzy or nauseous. At fifteen weeks your uterus isn't heavy enough for this to be a real concern, and if you wake up on your back, nothing bad has happened — just roll over.

But habits are easiest to build before they're needed, so this is your practice season. The classic setup: lie on your side — the left is the one most often mentioned, though either side is fine — with a pillow between your knees to keep your hips stacked and your lower back happy, and a pillow or small wedge tucked slightly under your bump as it grows. Some women swear by a full body pillow; others build the same fortress from ordinary pillows. And for stomach-sleepers: while it's still comfortable, sleeping on your stomach is generally considered fine in early and mid pregnancy — your body and your bump will retire that position naturally, with plenty of warning.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially heavy or with cramping · severe or one-sided pelvic pain that doesn't ease with rest · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine or dizziness · fainting · fluid leaking from the vagina. One congestion footnote: a cold that arrives with a high fever, or sinus pain that's severe or lingers well beyond a week, deserves a call too. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 15

  • Book the anatomy scan if it isn't booked — the usual window is weeks 18–22
  • Ask your doctor about second-trimester screening if you want it — typically offered around weeks 15–20, entirely your choice
  • Set up your sleep fortress and start the side-sleeping habit while it's easy
  • Assemble a nasal survival kit: saline spray, tissues, a bedside humidifier if the air is dry, and a soft toothbrush for tender gums
  • Start a tiny notebook or notes-app page for questions between appointments — the 2 a.m. ones are always forgotten by morning

Key takeaways

  • Your baby's retinas already respond to light, though the eyes stay shut
  • Hiccups and taste buds are beginning — a full-time training program in there
  • The stuffy nose is pregnancy rhinitis: saline helps; check before using medication
  • Side-sleeping practice season starts now, while building the habit is easy
