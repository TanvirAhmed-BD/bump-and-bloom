Pregnancy Week 37: Early Term — Almost There!

Welcome to week 37, mama — you have officially arrived at early term. From this week, your baby is no longer considered premature, and that is a milestone worth pausing on: the tiny poppy seed of week 4 is now a baby measuring about nineteen inches, practicing breathing, gripping, and sucking like a professional. But early term comes with its own suspenseful game: every twinge becomes a question. Was that it? Is this labor? Today we play detective together — your baby, your body, the mucus plug explained without the drama, how to tell early labor signs from the false alarms, and how to rest and nest your way through the final countdown. Here's your complete guide to week 37.

Your baby this week

Your baby now weighs about six and a third pounds and measures around nineteen inches long — about the size of a bunch of Swiss chard. Welcome to early term: babies born at 37 and 38 weeks are in this category — developed enough to meet the world, with just a little finishing left. Because here's the part worth knowing: these last weeks are not a waiting room.

They are a workshop. Your baby's brain is growing at one of the fastest rates of the entire pregnancy, the lungs are completing their final maturation, and the immune system is stocking up — your baby is receiving antibodies from you across the placenta, building the borrowed protection that will stand guard during their first weeks of life outside. The practice sessions are nearly performance-ready now.

Your baby rehearses breathing movements — drawing amniotic fluid in and out — preparing lungs that have never once used air. The grip is strong and deliberate; babies this age can grasp firmly, which means a certain little hand may soon wrap around your finger with surprising determination. Sucking and swallowing are coordinated as a team now.

And your baby keeps working on that final layer of fat, smoothing into the soft, round newborn shape — nearly there, but still adding the finishing touches day by day. Growth continues at a gentle, steady clip — roughly, many babies add about half a pound a week through this final stretch, though every baby keeps their own pace, and estimates of size are exactly that: estimates. You'll still feel hiccups, too — those small, rhythmic jumps — as the diaphragm rehearses along with everything else.

And the vernix coating your baby's skin is thickest around now, doing its protective work to the very end. Position-wise, most babies are head-down and low by now, settled into the pelvis or close to it, with movements that are all rolls, pushes, and stretches — the same familiar pattern, in the same familiar rhythm. That pattern, as you know by now, is gold: it should stay consistent right up to birth, whatever else changes. If your baby is still not head-down, your doctor will keep discussing the picture with you at your weekly visits — the plan is theirs to guide, and yours to ask questions about, for as long as it takes both of you to feel clear.

Your body & symptoms: living in the waiting room

And you, mama — week 37 feels like living in a beautiful, heavy waiting room. Pelvic pressure is often at its most insistent now, especially if your baby has dropped: a deep, carrying-low heaviness, sharp little nerve twinges, and a waddle that deserves its own theme music. Bathroom trips reach their series finale of frequency.

Braxton Hicks practice contractions may be stronger, longer, and frequent enough to make you stop and check the clock — we'll do the full labor-versus-rehearsal detective work in just a moment. Another common sight around now: an increase in vaginal discharge, sometimes with a new guest — the mucus plug. For most of pregnancy, a plug of thick mucus has sealed your cervix, standing guard between the outside world and your baby.

As your cervix begins to soften and prepare in these final weeks, that plug may come away — it can look like thick, jelly-like mucus, clear or slightly tinged with pink or brown. Losing it is a sign your body is preparing, not a starting gun: labor may follow in hours, days, or still a week or two later. We'll give it the full, calm explanation in the next section, including the one important distinction — the difference between a tinged mucus plug and actual bleeding, which is always a call.

Also on the week-37 roster: back and hip aches that have fully moved in, restless fragmented sleep, swelling in feet and hands, possible bursts of energy followed by equally impressive naps, and an emotional weather system all its own — excitement, impatience, tenderness, and the occasional 2 a.m. "am I really ready?" Yes, mama. You really are. The checklist that's left is short, and it's the next one we're about to do together: learning to read the signs your body is about to start sending more clearly.

Early labor signs vs false labor: the detective work

Time for the section to bookmark and revisit at midnight sometime in the next three weeks. Your body sends two kinds of contractions in this season, and learning their habits is how you'll know which is which. The rehearsals — Braxton Hicks — have a recognizable personality: they're irregular, with no steady pattern; they don't steadily grow stronger, longer, and closer together; and, most tellingly, they tend to ease off or stop when you change position, rest, drink some water, or take a warm shower.

Many women describe them as a tightening that comes and goes without building — noticeable, sometimes uncomfortable, but going nowhere in particular. Think of them as your uterus in training: real work, real preparation, not the main event. True early labor, by contrast, has a slow-building momentum.

Contractions become regular — a pattern you can time. They gradually grow stronger, last longer, and arrive closer together. And crucially, they keep coming whatever you do — resting, walking, and position changes don't switch them off.

Many women also notice the contraction sensation changing character: starting in the back and sweeping around to the front, or a deep cramping pressure low in the pelvis. If you recognize that pattern building, the move is simple: time them, and contact your doctor or birthplace, following the specific instructions your own team has given you for when to call — every practice words it a little differently, and their version is the one that counts for you. Then, the supporting cast of signs.

The mucus plug, as we said: thick, jelly-like mucus, possibly tinged pink or brown — a sign of preparation, not an emergency, and worth mentioning at your next call or visit rather than racing anywhere. You may also hear the phrase "bloody show" — a small amount of blood-tinged mucus as the cervix changes; a small amount mixed with mucus generally belongs to the preparation story. But make this distinction bright and clear: bleeding that is bright red, flowing, or anything like a period is a different matter entirely — that is always a call right away, at any hour.

And your water breaking: a gush, or a slow trickle you can't stop, is always a call to your doctor right away, contractions or no contractions — and especially if the fluid looks green or brown, or has a strong smell. When in doubt about any of it — genuinely, any of it — call and describe it. "I'm not sure" is a perfectly good reason. It's most of the reasons, honestly, in week 37.

Resting & nesting: the final weeks' balance

So how do you live inside the suspense without climbing the walls? Two words get equal billing this week: nesting, and resting — and the art is holding them in balance. Nest if it brings you joy: the folded clothes, the stocked freezer, the hospital bag by the door are all genuinely useful, and there's a particular pleasure in a house that's ready.

But keep it gentle and keep it short — tasks at counter height, not on ladders; projects measured in minutes, not renovations. Your body is weeks from the biggest athletic event of its life. Nobody trains for a marathon by repainting the stadium.

Rest like it's your job, because it is: naps without apology, feet up, early nights, and a strict filter on your information diet — this is a beautiful week to step back from other people's birth stories, the dramatic ones especially, and keep your own story unwritten and unhurried. Gentle daily movement, if it feels good, keeps circulation and mood up: a walk around the block, some stretching, a swim if you enjoy it. And soak up the strange, suspended sweetness of these days when you can — the meals out, the quiet evenings, the hand resting on a belly that will not be a belly much longer.

This waiting is not empty time, mama. It's the last page of the chapter, and it deserves to be read slowly.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Your water breaking leads the list this week: a gush or a continuous trickle you can’t control — a call right away, contractions or not, and if the fluid looks green or brown or has a strong smell, say so on that call. Regular contractions that are strengthening and settling into a pattern: time them, and call your doctor or birthplace following their guidance for you — sooner if anything on this list joins them. Also call right away for: a clear decrease in your baby’s movements, or a pattern that feels different from their normal — space is snug, but the pattern is law, and it does not retire at week 37 · bleeding that is bright red, flowing, or like a period — as distinct from the small amount of blood-tinged mucus that can come with preparation · the preeclampsia signs: a severe headache that doesn’t ease with rest · vision changes like spots, blurring, or flashing lights · sudden swelling of your face or hands · pain high in your belly, especially under your right ribs · sudden, rapid weight gain · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher. And call emergency services for severe breathlessness or chest pain, which are never wait-and-see symptoms, in pregnancy or out of it. Most moms meet none of this. But every mom should know it — because early calls are how small questions stay small, and your team would far rather get the call.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 37

  • Learn the detective work: false labor eases with rest, water, and position changes; true labor builds a pattern and won’t switch off
  • When a pattern builds, time the contractions — and follow your own team’s instructions for when to call
  • Know the bright line: a tinged mucus plug means preparation, but bright-red, flowing bleeding is always a call right away
  • Hospital bag by the door, clothes folded, freezer stocked — the short final checklist
  • Nest gently and briefly: tasks at counter height, not on ladders; projects measured in minutes, not renovations
