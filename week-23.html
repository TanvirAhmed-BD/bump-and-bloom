Pregnancy Week 23: Your Baby Can Hear Your Voice Clearly

Welcome to week 23, mama — the week your voice becomes your baby's favorite sound in the world. Your baby's hearing has sharpened beautifully, and the conversations, songs, and stories you share now may be remembered after birth. Today we also look at the lungs' quiet, crucial construction project, talk honestly and carefully about a milestone called viability, and check in on the swelling, snoring, and forgetfulness of week 23. Here's your complete guide.

Your baby this week

Your baby now measures about eleven and a half inches and weighs just over one pound — about the size of a large grapefruit, and steadily outgrowing fruit-basket comparisons in every direction except charm. Their skin is gradually becoming less translucent as fat slowly begins to build beneath it, though "slowly" is the honest word: the great filling-out is still ahead, and your baby will gain weight rapidly in the coming weeks. The ears are this week's stars.

The structures of the inner ear have matured to the point where your baby hears the world with real clarity — your heartbeat, the whoosh of your blood, the gurgles of your digestion, and above it all, your voice, carried through your body in a way no other sound can match. Loud noises may startle your baby into a jump you'll feel; gentler sounds — you reading aloud, humming, talking to the bump — become part of their daily landscape. Babies in studies recognize their mother's voice and even familiar melodies and stories after birth, which means the lullaby you sing now may genuinely soothe your newborn later.

Down in the chest, the lungs are on a quiet, vital mission: the smallest airways are branching, the beginnings of air sacs are forming, tiny blood vessels are weaving through the lung tissue, and cells are starting to produce surfactant, the substance that will one day help those air sacs stay open for breathing air. There's a long way to go — the lungs are among the last systems to fully mature — but the construction is well underway. The skeleton is keeping pace, bones hardening from their cartilage beginnings and drawing calcium steadily, and the face looks increasingly like the one you'll meet: eyes in place beneath still-fused lids, little nostrils formed, lips and chin distinct.

The diaphragm has begun its own quiet practice, too — small rhythmic movements that will later show up as hiccups. And movement by now can be gloriously dramatic: kicks are strong, rolls and somersaults are still on the menu, and many women begin seeing ripples and pushes move across the belly itself, as your baby's sleep and wake cycles grow more defined.

A careful word about viability

Around 23 to 24 weeks is what doctors call the threshold of viability: the stage at which a baby born very early can sometimes survive with intensive care in a neonatal unit. Every single day and week of continued growth improves those chances significantly.

Hold the milestone this way: it describes how far your baby has come, not a prediction about your pregnancy — the overwhelming majority of babies at 23 weeks stay safely inside for many more weeks, which is exactly where they grow best. Your part in the story doesn't change at all: keep your appointments, and call promptly for anything on the warning-signs list below.

Your body & symptoms: swelling, snoring & pregnancy brain

Your bump is high and round, your uterus well above your belly button, and clothes have fully reorganized themselves around the pregnancy. Appetite is generally hearty in this stretch — your baby is funding serious construction — and most doctors expect a steady climb in weight rather than any perfect number, roughly the pace you discussed together at the start. The week-23 regulars: mild swelling of feet, ankles, and fingers, worse by evening and in the heat; backache as your balance point migrates; leg cramps on the night shift; Braxton Hicks practice tightenings, which you are now an expert at recognizing — irregular, easing with rest and water; and skin changes from stretch marks to a darkening belly line to patches of dryness or itch.

Two charming additions arrive for many women around now. First, snoring: congestion from all that extra blood volume swells the nasal passages, and many partners report the soundtrack with great amusement. Second, forgetfulness — the famous pregnancy brain.

Keys in the fridge, sentences that evaporate mid-way, appointments that need three reminders. Mild forgetfulness is extremely common and genuinely not a sign of anything wrong; your brain is running an enormous background project.

Lists and phone reminders are not defeat — they are strategy. Also still possible: heartburn after bigger meals, occasional lightheadedness if you rise too fast, and a darkening or widening of the areolas as your body quietly prepares for feeding.

Talking, singing & bonding this week

Since hearing is the theme, here is the loveliest homework of pregnancy: talking to your baby. No special equipment, no right words. Narrate the ordinary — what you're making for dinner, where you're walking, what the dog just did.

Read a favorite book aloud in the evening. Sing the songs you'd like to be family songs someday, because familiarity is being built right now, note by note. Partners sometimes feel one step removed from the whole experience, and this is a beautiful door in: a few minutes of talking or reading to the bump each day, a hand resting there during your baby's busy hour, and the reward is often an answering kick.

Siblings, too — a big brother or sister announcing the day's news to the baby is the kind of memory families retell for decades. One sensible note on volume: your baby hears the world muffled and filtered, and your everyday sounds — conversation, music at normal levels, the ordinary noise of life — are all part of the healthy landscape. There's no need for headphones on the belly or special speakers; your voice, arriving the natural way, is already the main event.

Many parents pick one song and sing or play it regularly in these weeks — choose something you will still love at 3 a.m. in month two. You are, in a very real sense, programming the future's lullaby.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Contractions that become regular, stronger, or painful · any vaginal bleeding · any leaking of fluid · a feeling of pelvic pressure pressing down · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · painful or burning urination · a severe headache · any change in your vision · sudden swelling of your face or hands, or rapid overall swelling. And movement: your baby's pattern is becoming more recognizable each week — you know the busy hours by now. If you notice a clear, sustained decrease from your baby's normal — not a quiet afternoon, but a real change — call the same day and say exactly that. Early calls are how teams help best, and nobody will ever think less of you for making one.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 23

  • Start voice time: a few minutes of talking, reading, or singing to the bump daily — and invite your partner in
  • Support circulation: a daily walk, ankle circles whenever you sit, feet up in the evening, water within reach all day
  • Get the glucose screening on the calendar for the 24–28 week window, and ask whether you should fast before yours
  • Start the gentle homework: pediatrician recommendations from friends, and a birthplace tour date on the calendar
  • Keep one master list on your phone — questions for your doctor, things to buy, things to ask — to outsmart the forgetfulness
  • Keep up the prenatal vitamin and the bump photos, and take one proper rest with your feet up

Key takeaways

  • Your baby hears with real clarity now — and your voice is the favorite sound
  • The lungs are branching and beginning surfactant; the great filling-out is ahead
  • Viability (around 23–24 weeks) marks how far baby has come — not a prediction about your pregnancy
  • Book the glucose screening for the 24–28 week window, and ask about fasting
