Pregnancy Week 27: Welcome to the Third Trimester Soon

Welcome to week 27, mama — the final week of your second trimester, and your baby is celebrating with a schedule of their own. Your little one now sleeps and wakes in real cycles, hiccups make regular guest appearances, and your dreams — well, your dreams have gone full cinema. Today we'll look at your baby's rhythms, your body's week-27 roster, and exactly what changes when the third trimester begins next week. Here's your complete guide to closing out this trimester in style.

Your baby this week

Your baby now measures about fourteen and a half inches and weighs around two pounds — roughly the size of a head of cabbage, and continuing the produce-aisle tour with great commitment. Beneath the comparisons, the real story is rhythm. Your baby sleeps and wakes in genuine cycles now. During sleep, the eyes move beneath their newly opened lids — the rapid-eye-movement stage, the dream stage — so in all likelihood your baby is already dreaming, in a world made of warmth, rhythm, and your voice.

During wakeful stretches, the program is kicks, stretches, rolls, and the season's recurring special guest: hiccups. Those small, rhythmic, jumping pulses that repeat for a few minutes are your baby's diaphragm practicing, and they're a normal, healthy part of development — many moms end up finding them funny, then oddly comforting.

The senses are nearly complete. Hearing is sharp; your baby knows your voice from every other sound in the world. The brain is in the middle of its great wrinkling: all through this trimester turn, the surface of your baby's brain folds into grooves and ridges, multiplying the space available for the connections that will one day carry language, memory, and personality. Those connections are forming at a pace that will never be matched again in your baby's entire life — hundreds of new neural links every minute, built from the raw materials your body delivers.

And here's a detail parents love: your baby can now respond differently to familiar and unfamiliar things — settling at your voice, stirring at a new sound, quieting when you rest a warm hand on the bump and hold it there. The recognition system that will help your newborn find you in a crowded room is already installed and taking notes.

The eyes open and close and respond to light. Taste buds are experienced now, seasoned daily by your menu. The lungs continue their patient construction — air sacs multiplying, surfactant building — and fat is layering on in earnest.

Position note: your baby still has room to move and change positions freely, and many do — head up, head down, sideways, diagonally, all in a day's work. There's no deadline yet for settling head-down; that story develops over the coming weeks. And your baby's grip, tested on the cord and their own hands for weeks now, is strong enough that it will surprise the first person who offers a finger in the delivery room.

Your body & symptoms: dreams, cramps & the full roster

And you, mama — your body is closing out the trimester with a full roster. Vivid dreams headline for many women: sprawling, emotional, movie-length features starring the baby, the birth, and occasionally a talking refrigerator. They're fueled by hormones, lighter and more interrupted sleep, and a brain processing an enormous life event. Strange dreams are a pregnancy classic, not a prophecy — and if anxiety dreams are visiting, you're in extremely normal company. Talking them out — with your partner, a friend, or your doctor if they weigh on you — shrinks them back to size. They are weather, not forecasts.

Leg cramps continue their night raids — the calf cramp that launches you upright at 3 a.m. remains a rite of passage; the rescue is the same as ever: straighten the leg, flex the foot, pull your toes up toward your shin. Heartburn keeps its evening residency, managed best with smaller meals and staying upright after eating. Shortness of breath makes lighter appearances as your uterus climbs — your lungs have less expansion room, and breathlessness on stairs or mid-sentence is common and expected.

Backache hums along at its established volume. Belly skin itches as it stretches, mild swelling visits your feet and hands by evening, and Braxton Hicks tightenings arrive irregularly and depart with rest and water. Also possible now: hemorrhoids joining the cast — pressure and blood volume again — where fiber-rich food, plenty of water, and not straining are the standard comforts, with your doctor able to suggest more if they're troublesome.

And a warm note: many women feel a burst of nesting energy around this turn — urges to organize, prepare, and fold impossibly small clothes. Enjoy it in sensible doses. The ceiling fan does not need deep-cleaning. It never did.

Next week: the third trimester — what changes

Let's preview what actually changes when week 28 opens the third trimester, so nothing catches you by surprise. First, appointments: many doctors move to about every two weeks from around week 28 until about week 36, and then weekly until the birth. Each visit keeps the same reassuring rhythm — blood pressure, urine, weight, belly growth, heartbeat, and later, the baby's position — a closer safety net as the birth approaches.

Second, movement awareness becomes more formal. Your baby's pattern — their busy hours and quiet hours — becomes something doctors ask about and take seriously, and you'll hear more about noticing changes promptly. You've been learning that pattern for weeks already; the third trimester simply promotes your expertise to official status.

Third, a few conversations typically happen in this window, all with your own doctor, who knows your history: if your blood type is Rh-negative, there's a routine protective injection around 28 weeks; many doctors also recommend the Tdap vaccine against whooping cough in this stretch so protection passes to your baby, and a flu shot in season. These are standard agenda items, not alarms — bring your questions and decide together.

Appetite and energy deserve a line too: many women notice hunger arriving in earnest now — your baby is about to enter the fastest weight-gain phase of the whole pregnancy — and smaller, more frequent meals continue to outsmart the heartburn, while energy may begin its slow third-trimester taper; plan your big efforts for your best hours of the day rather than fighting the tide.

And finally, the practical preparations begin in earnest: thinking through your birth preferences — our Birth Plan post in this series is built for exactly that — hospital or birthplace registration if it's offered, the hospital bag, finalizing your pediatrician choice, and the home-front logistics: a car seat researched before it's needed, a route to the birthplace you've actually driven once, a short list of humans who know they're on call and for what, a stocked freezer corner if your household runs that way. None of it needs doing this week. All of it gets easier when it starts gently, now, rather than urgently, later.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

A severe headache that doesn't ease · vision changes — spots, blurring, flashing lights · sudden swelling of your face or hands · pain high in your belly, especially under your right ribs — these can be signs of preeclampsia and always deserve a prompt call · any vaginal bleeding · any leaking of fluid · regular, stronger, or painful contractions before 37 weeks · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · painful urination. And movement, with new emphasis: as you cross into the third trimester, your knowledge of your baby's pattern becomes one of the most valuable safety tools in the whole pregnancy. If you notice a clear decrease from your baby's normal pattern, call the same day and say so plainly. Do not wait until tomorrow to see. Your care team would rather check a healthy baby a hundred times than miss the one time it mattered. And for severe breathlessness or chest pain, skip the phone tree entirely — those are emergency-services symptoms, always.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 27

  • Mark the transition — take the bump photo, note the milestone, and give yourself ten honest seconds of pride: two trimesters of growing a person is an achievement
  • Assemble your questions for the coming every-two-weeks era; visit frequency is about to double, and your list will get used
  • Start the gentle versions of the big preparations — a draft list of hospital bag items, birth preferences to discuss, pediatrician shortlist. Drafts, not deadlines
  • Keep the anti-cramp, anti-heartburn toolkit running: calf stretches before bed, smaller evening meals, water all day, feet up when they offer themselves
  • Keep talking to your baby during their wakeful hours and enjoy the hiccup concerts
  • Guard your rest like it's a prenatal vitamin, because functionally, it is one

Key takeaways

  • Your baby sleeps and wakes in real cycles — and, with REM sleep, is in all likelihood already dreaming
  • Hiccups are diaphragm practice: a normal, healthy part of development
  • Third trimester preview: visits about every two weeks, and movement awareness turns official
  • Start gentle drafts now — hospital bag list, birth preferences, pediatrician shortlist
