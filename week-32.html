Pregnancy Week 32: Your Baby Is Practicing Breathing

Welcome to week 32, mama — and take a quiet moment for this headline: your baby is now practicing breathing. In, out, in, out — the muscles of breathing rehearsing for the very first breath of air on birthday morning. This week also brings fully formed toenails, a body that's gaining weight beautifully, and for you: checkups every two weeks, heartburn on tour, and the gentle nudge that it's hospital-bag season. We'll cover your baby, your body, your visits, the bag, and the warning signs. Here's your complete guide to week 32.

Your baby this week

Your baby now weighs close to four pounds — about a kilo and three quarters — and measures nearly seventeen inches long: about the size of a squash. And those breathing practice sessions are the week's featured event. Your baby rhythmically moves the muscles of breathing, drawing amniotic fluid in and out of the developing lungs. It's not breathing air — oxygen still arrives courtesy of you, through the placenta — but it's precisely the rehearsal that makes the first real breaths possible.

Babies have been practicing this since mid-pregnancy; by now, the practice is organized and regular, and sometimes you can even see the gentle, rhythmic movement on your belly.

The finishing school details continue. Your baby's toenails are now fully formed — fingernails arrived a while ago — and the bones throughout the body are fully formed too, though still softer than they'll be after birth, with the skull bones remaining deliberately flexible for the journey out. Under the skin, fat keeps accumulating in earnest: your baby will gain roughly half a pound a week from here, plumping toward newborn roundness and building the insulation and energy reserves for life outside. The skin itself looks smoother and less translucent every week.

Your baby's senses are busy taking in the world — listening to you, sensing light, tasting the day's menu. Sleep cycles are more defined now, with quiet sleep, active sleep, and wakeful periods rotating, and your baby may have settled into favorite positions, spending increasing time head-down as space tightens — though many babies are still happily breech or sideways at 32 weeks, with weeks left to turn; position checks become a topic at later visits, not a worry for today.

One more quiet transfer is picking up pace around now: antibodies from your immune system are crossing the placenta to your baby, in amounts that grow as the weeks pass — your body's protection, on loan to theirs. And up top, your baby's hair may be growing in earnest now, anywhere from a light fuzz to a genuine head of hair waiting to surprise everyone in the delivery room. Even the eyebrows and eyelashes are in place. The portrait is filling in.

Your body & symptoms: the squeeze is real

Your week-32 inventory, from the inside: your uterus reaches high under your ribs now, and the squeeze explains the week's greatest hits. Heartburn — the stomach, sharing a cramped neighborhood, protests especially after meals and at bedtime. Shortness of breath during ordinary activities, as your diaphragm works in reduced space. Back and pelvic aches; Braxton Hicks tightenings, now possibly several times a day; and the return of the frequent bathroom trips, because bladder space is at a historic low.

Also appearing: itchy skin across the stretch; mild swelling in feet and ankles; leg cramps and restless legs at night; colostrum leaks for some moms; hemorrhoids or varicose veins for some, courtesy of blood volume and pressure; and a waddle-adjacent walk that your hips have chosen for sound logistical reasons. Sleep is fragmented — heartburn plus bathroom trips plus a brain rehearsing parenthood will do that — and vivid dreams remain a house specialty.

And the movement note for this week: your baby's kicks and rolls should stay strong and frequent. The choreography changes as space tightens — more rolls and stretches, fewer somersaults — but the amount and pattern should feel familiar. That's the thread to hold onto, and we'll pick it up again in the warning signs.

Emotionally, week 32 often splits in two: half of you is nesting with project-manager intensity, and half of you would like a nap that lasts until the due date. Both halves are correct. Many women also notice the questions from the outside world changing — fewer “how are you feeling?” and more “still pregnant?” — usually asked of a woman holding a four-pound baby internally, standing up. Smile, hydrate, and know that the only correct due-date countdown is the one happening on your doctor's chart.

Your appointments: the every-two-weeks rhythm

By now, your prenatal visits have likely settled into the every-two-weeks rhythm that runs from about week 28 to about week 36, when they usually become weekly. Each visit follows a reassuring routine: blood pressure and urine checked — the quiet screen for preeclampsia — weight and belly growth measured, baby's heartbeat heard, and increasingly, your doctor feeling for your baby's position and asking about movement, contractions, and how you're actually doing. That last question counts. Use it — for the swelling question, the sleep question, the 2 a.m. anxiety question. All of them are legitimate agenda items.

These visits are also decision season. If you haven't yet, this is a natural window to talk through birth preferences — our Birth Plan post in this series walks you through the options — to ask about pain-relief approaches at your birthplace, and to discuss when your doctor wants you to call and then come in once labor begins; every practice has its own guidance, and knowing yours replaces guesswork with a plan. It's also a good time to ask what happens if baby decides to arrive early or late, purely as orientation.

And keep this habit going: ask exactly how your doctor wants you tracking movement, and follow their method — your pattern knowledge plus their guidance is the safety net working as designed.

A small habit that pays off at every one of these visits: keep a running note on your phone — symptoms, questions, the thing you meant to ask at 2 a.m. — because the famous pregnancy brain will absolutely delete it by the time you're in the exam room. No question is too small for this list.

One more practical item: hospital registration or pre-registration, if your birthplace offers it, is paperwork best done now, calmly, rather than between contractions. And if your partner or support person can join one of these visits, this is a lovely stretch for it: they hear the heartbeat, meet the team who may be there on the day, and absorb the “when do we call, when do we come in” guidance firsthand. Two people who both know the plan is worth more than one person remembering it under pressure.

The hospital bag: pack it like a pro

Which brings us to the beloved ritual of week 32: the hospital bag. Packing it this week — and leaving it by the door or in the car — buys you a calm departure day whenever baby chooses it, and it's genuinely satisfying nesting work.

The essentials, in brief: for you, documents and your birth plan copies, comfortable clothes and a robe, toiletries, lip balm, hair ties, phone charger with a long cable, snacks and drinks for after, and nursing-friendly tops if you plan to breastfeed. For your partner or support person: a change of clothes, snacks, and their own charger. For baby: a going-home outfit in newborn and zero-to-three sizes — babies vary — a blanket, and the car seat, installed and checked ahead of time rather than debuted in the parking lot.

We have a whole post in this series that walks through the complete checklist item by item — our Hospital Bag post — so give it a read and pack alongside it. The short version of its philosophy: pack for comfort, pack for the partner, and remember that hospitals supply more than you think. The bag doesn't need to survive a month in the wilderness; it needs to make two or three days feel cared for.

And a gentle word on what to leave out: the “everything I own, just in case” approach produces a bag you'll curse at the admissions desk. Skip the suitcase of pillows — ask your birthplace what they provide, because many supply more than you'd expect, from gowns to basic toiletries to baby blankets for the stay. Pack the things that make it feel like yours: your robe, your lip balm, your music, your snacks, that one going-home outfit you've already photographed in your mind ten times. Comfort is personal, not voluminous.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Preterm labor, meaning labor before 37 weeks: regular contractions that keep coming, strengthen, or get closer together · pelvic pressure, like the baby pushing down · a dull low backache that won't ease · menstrual-like cramps · a change in vaginal discharge — watery, mucus-like, or bloody · any vaginal bleeding · leaking fluid that might be your water breaking — a call right away, day or night. Movement: a clear decrease in your baby's movements, or a pattern that feels different from your baby's normal — call the same day. Not the next appointment, not tomorrow — today. Preeclampsia, screened at every visit and watched between them: a severe headache that doesn't ease with rest · vision changes — spots, blurring, flashing lights · sudden swelling of your face or hands · pain high in your belly, especially under your right ribs · sudden, rapid weight gain · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · painful urination. And call emergency services for chest pain or severe breathlessness. Most moms never make one of these calls. But they're the reason the checkups, the kick counting, and this list exist: to catch the few situations where speed changes everything — with a team that would always rather hear from you early.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 32

  • Pack the hospital bag this week and park it by the door or in the car
  • Install the car seat and check it ahead of time — not for the first time in the hospital parking lot
  • Keep a running note on your phone: symptoms, questions, the thing you meant to ask at 2 a.m.
  • Confirm exactly how your doctor wants you tracking movement, and follow their method
  • Talk through birth preferences, pain-relief approaches, and when your doctor wants you to call and come in
  • Hospital registration or pre-registration, if your birthplace offers it — paperwork done calmly now
  • Bring your partner to one of these visits if you can: two people who both know the plan beat one remembering it under pressure

Key takeaways

  • Breathing practice is organized and regular now — the rehearsal that makes the first real breaths possible
  • Toenails are complete, and your baby will gain roughly half a pound a week from here
  • Visits run every two weeks until about week 36: bring the 2 a.m. questions — they're all agenda items
  • It's hospital-bag season: pack it this week, park it by the door, and relax
