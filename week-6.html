Pregnancy Week 6: First Heartbeat Flicker

Your baby this week

Sweet-pea sized, and busy: the heart is beating in a regular rhythm, tiny buds that become arms and legs are appearing, and the early brain, eyes and ears are under construction. The head is still large compared with the body — the brain is the flagship project right now. If you have an early ultrasound this week, that flickering pixel is the moment most parents never forget.

Your body & symptoms: nausea joins the team

Morning sickness typically arrives in earnest around week 6 (blame rapidly rising hCG and estrogen). Add tender breasts, fatigue, frequent bathroom trips, bloating and a sense of smell that could win awards. If you don't have nausea, that can be perfectly normal too — symptoms vary enormously from mom to mom and pregnancy to pregnancy.

Eating when everything smells unbearable

Small, frequent, bland and cold foods often go down better than big hot meals (less cooking smell). Keep plain crackers by the bed for before you get up, sip fluids steadily through the day, and let "good enough" nutrition be enough for now — the first trimester is about getting through, not eating perfectly.

Rest is your job right now

Your body is building a placenta and a person while running your normal life. Early nights, naps when you can grab them, and saying no without guilt are not indulgences — they're part of the work.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Heavy bleeding · severe pain · fainting · a fever · vomiting that leaves you unable to keep fluids down for 24 hours. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 6

  • Keep plain crackers by the bed for before you get up
  • Choose small, cold, bland meals over big hot ones while smells are unbearable
  • Sip fluids steadily through the day
  • Protect your rest — early nights and naps are part of the work, not indulgences

Key takeaways

  • Your baby's heart is beating steadily; a first ultrasound may show the flicker
  • Morning sickness usually arrives around week 6 — and its absence can be normal too
  • Small, cold, bland meals and steady sips beat three big meals
  • Rest is genuinely part of your job in the first trimester
