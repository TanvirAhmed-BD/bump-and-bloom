Pregnancy Week 21: Your Baby Can Taste!

Welcome to week 21, mama — the week your baby starts joining you for dinner. Your little one now has working taste buds and is swallowing the fluid around them every day, which means the flavors of your meals are already on the menu. We'll also celebrate kicks that are getting stronger, check in on the heartburn and vein troubles of the middle months, and gather the simple comforts that actually help. Here's your complete guide to week 21.

Your baby this week

Your baby is stretching out impressively. Measured from head to heel now, they're about ten and a half inches long — roughly the size of a large carrot — and weigh around twelve ounces, a bit over three hundred grams. The headline system this week is digestion.

Your baby swallows amniotic fluid regularly now — real practice for feeding after birth — and it passes through their developing digestive tract, helping it mature. The byproduct of all that swallowing is meconium, your baby's first bowel substance, quietly accumulating in the intestines for its memorable debut in the first diapers. And those swallows carry flavor: your baby's taste buds are formed and functioning, and the fluid takes on hints of what you've been eating — the garlic, the spices, the sweetness.

Researchers believe this early tasting may be one reason babies later show interest in flavors their mothers ate often during pregnancy. So your varied, colorful meals are, in a small and lovely way, your baby's first food education. Elsewhere, production is shifting departments: your baby's bone marrow has begun making red blood cells, a job the liver and spleen handled earlier.

The kidneys are processing fluid and producing urine, which becomes part of the amniotic fluid. Eyebrows and eyelids are fully formed now, though the eyes remain fused shut a while longer. A creamy coating called vernix is spreading across the skin, with fine, soft lanugo helping hold it in place.

And the movements — what began as flutters is becoming a coordinated repertoire of kicks, twists, stretches, and somersaults. You may begin spotting busy times and quiet times: the earliest hints of a sleep-and-wake rhythm.

Your body & symptoms: the mid-pregnancy cast

Your own body has settled into an unmistakably pregnant rhythm. Your bump is out and proud, your uterus sits above your belly button, and steady weight gain is the plan — your baby has a lot of growing left to fund. Many women feel genuinely good in this stretch: energy is often decent, and the kicks are a daily delight.

But week 21 has its regular cast of discomforts too. Heartburn and indigestion top the list for many: pregnancy hormones relax the valve at the top of your stomach while your growing uterus presses upward, so rich, spicy, or simply large meals may send a familiar burn up your chest. Varicose veins may make an appearance as well — all that extra blood volume plus pressure from the uterus can make veins in the legs, and sometimes uncomfortable hemorrhoids, more prominent.

They're extremely common and usually calm down after birth. Also on the roster: leg cramps, especially at night; stretch marks making their first appearance for many, written mostly in your genetics; an itchy belly as the skin stretches; a darkening line down your stomach; the occasional mild swelling of feet by evening; and the beginnings of backache as your center of gravity keeps migrating forward.

Your emotional weather deserves a mention, too: for many women this middle stretch brings a steadier mood, strong cravings, and loosening food aversions. And if your mood instead feels persistently low or anxious, that matters as much as any physical symptom — tell your doctor, because support exists and it works.

Comforts: heartburn & veins

Let's gather the comforts that actually help, starting with heartburn. The big three: smaller meals, eaten more often; staying upright for a good while after eating instead of reclining on the couch; and propping your head and shoulders up at night if the burn visits after dark. Notice your personal triggers — for many women it's very spicy food, tomato-rich sauces, citrus, coffee on an empty stomach, or a large meal late at night — and adjust gently rather than banning half the cookbook.

One more ally: the after-meal walk. Ten or fifteen gentle minutes after eating helps digestion along, and it doubles as circulation care for your legs. Loose waistbands help too, and many women find that sipping water through the day beats flooding meals with big drinks.

If heartburn is fierce or constant, mention it at your next visit; your doctor can suggest pregnancy-appropriate options. Don't suffer in heroic silence. For your veins and circulation, movement is the theme: avoid standing still or sitting in one position for long stretches; when you must sit, circle your ankles and pump your feet like you're pressing pedals.

Short walks through the day keep blood moving upward, and put your feet up whenever the chance appears — truly up, above heart level if you can manage it. Some women find support stockings helpful; that's a good one to discuss with your doctor. And a note of reassurance: mild vein changes in pregnancy are common and usually improve after your baby arrives.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Any vaginal bleeding · any leaking of fluid · contractions or tightenings that become regular, rhythmical, or painful, or new pelvic pressure low down · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · painful or burning urination · severe abdominal pain · vomiting that won't stop · a severe headache · any change in your vision · sudden swelling of your face or hands — that suddenness is what separates it from the mild ankle puffiness of a long day. One more: your baby's movement pattern is still forming at 21 weeks, so don't hold yourself to counting rules yet. But you are the world's leading expert on your baby's usual busy times, and if you notice a clear, sustained decrease from that normal, call and say so. No doctor will ever be annoyed by that call.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 21

  • Bring any questions from your anatomy scan to your next appointment — no question is too small
  • Keep a running notes list of questions between visits, so the 2 a.m. questions don't vanish by morning
  • Begin gentle pelvic floor exercises (Kegels) if you haven't — a few minutes spread through the day
  • Moisturize the itchy belly as much as feels good, stay hydrated through the day, and keep taking your prenatal vitamin
  • Take one photo of the bump this week, and start a playful first draft of your essentials list

Key takeaways

  • Taste buds are working — your baby is sampling the flavors of your meals with every swallow
  • Heartburn relief: smaller meals, stay upright after eating, prop your head and shoulders up at night
  • For veins: move often, pump your ankles, walk daily, and put your feet up high
  • No movement-counting rules yet at 21 weeks — but a clear, sustained decrease from your baby's usual pattern deserves a call
