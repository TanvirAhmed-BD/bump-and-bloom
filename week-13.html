Pregnancy Week 13: Welcome to the Second Trimester!

Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of a lemon — roughly seven and a half centimeters, about three inches from head to bottom, and weighing around twenty-five grams. The head, once half the entire body, is now about a third of your baby's size as the body catches up.

The headline act is on the tiniest stage: the swirling ridges that will become fingerprints are forming on your baby's fingertips — one-of-a-kind patterns they'll carry for life. The vocal cords are developing, the intestines are moving into the abdomen and beginning to work, and the kidneys produce urine that's released into the amniotic fluid, which your baby also swallows — the digestive system quietly practicing.

The skeleton, soft cartilage until now, is beginning to harden into bone, the tooth buds for all twenty baby teeth are in place, and the placenta is now fully formed and on duty as your baby's lifeline. At many visits around now, your doctor can find the heartbeat quickly with a handheld Doppler — that fast, galloping rhythm is often the moment the pregnancy starts feeling like a person.

Your body & symptoms: the fog lifts

The placenta has taken over hormone production, and as those levels level off, the nausea that ruled your mornings often fades, the bone-deep tiredness loosens, and food starts tasting like food again. If that hasn't happened for you yet, hang on — for many women the shift arrives over the next couple of weeks, and still feeling rough at thirteen weeks is common and normal too.

Your uterus has grown up and out of your pelvis, and you may notice the very beginnings of a bump — more "my jeans have opinions" than obvious pregnancy. Breasts may feel less tender, clear or milky discharge is typical, and constipation and heartburn may linger even as the nausea leaves — fiber, fluids, and gentle walks are still your best tools.

A quieter mind: why the risk drops now

The milestone underneath the milestone: most miscarriages happen in the first trimester, and the large majority are caused by chromosomal differences present from the moment of conception — nothing you ate, lifted, worried about, or did caused them. By week thirteen — especially once you've seen your baby and that flickering heartbeat at a scan — the chance of miscarriage has dropped considerably compared with the early weeks.

Two honest footnotes: "much lower" is not "zero," and the warning signs below still matter. And if anxiety is still riding along with you, you are not doing pregnancy wrong — mentioning it at your next appointment is a strength, not a complaint. But if you've been waiting for permission to exhale a little: consider this it. It's also why many families choose this week to share their news more widely — though every timing is the right one if it feels right to you.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially if heavy or with cramping · severe or one-sided pelvic or shoulder pain · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine or dizziness · fainting · fluid leaking from the vagina. Trust yourself — making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 13

  • Keep up your prenatal vitamin — folic acid is still doing important work
  • Ask when your anatomy scan will be booked (usually weeks 18–22 — popular slots fill up)
  • Gently rebuild movement as energy returns — walking, swimming, prenatal yoga — with your doctor's blessing
  • Book that dental checkup if it slipped during the nauseous months, and tell your dentist you're expecting
  • Do one fun thing: tell a favorite person, take a bump photo, start the list of names

Key takeaways

  • Fingerprints are forming, and the placenta is fully formed and on duty
  • Symptoms often ease now as the placenta takes over hormone production
  • The risk of miscarriage drops considerably after the first trimester
  • Book ahead: the anatomy scan window is roughly weeks 18–22
