Pregnancy Week 16: Welcome to the Second Trimester

Welcome, officially, to the second trimester — the stretch of pregnancy so many women call the golden one. Your energy is likely returning, your bump is starting to introduce itself, and your avocado-sized baby is beginning to sense light and sound from the world outside. Here's your complete guide to week 16.

Your baby this week

Your baby measures about eleven to twelve centimeters — roughly four and a half inches from head to bottom — and weighs around one hundred grams: a small avocado, sitting perfectly in your palm. And this little avocado is getting remarkably capable. The eyes, still sealed shut, have become sensitive to light — shine a bright light on your belly and your baby may actually turn away from it.

The ears are nearly in their final position, and the tiny bones inside them are hardening; over the coming weeks your baby will start picking up muffled sounds — the whoosh of your blood flow, your heartbeat, and eventually, your voice. The arms and legs now move in a coordinated way, bending at elbows and knees, and the hands can find each other — some babies are already practicing thumb-sucking.

The pattern of hair on the scalp is set, the skeleton continues its slow change from rubbery cartilage to bone, and the legs have grown longer than the arms, so your baby finally looks in proportion. At appointments, your doctor can usually find that fast, galloping heartbeat quickly with a handheld Doppler — often the highlight of the visit.

Your body & symptoms: energy returns

For many moms, week sixteen feels like the tide has truly turned. The placenta is fully on duty, hormone levels have steadied, and the nausea and deep fatigue of the first trimester often fade into something much more manageable. Appetites return — sometimes with enthusiasm. Your uterus has grown well up out of the pelvis, and most women now have a visible bump, though its size varies enormously from body to body and pregnancy to pregnancy — all normal.

New sensations to expect: round ligament pain, the famous one — a brief, sharp twinge or ache low on one or both sides of your belly when you stand up quickly, cough, sneeze, or roll over in bed. It passes in seconds or minutes, eases when you rest or change position, and is considered a normal part of pregnancy's remodeling. Also common now: a stuffy nose or even nosebleeds, the beginnings of backache as your center of gravity shifts, skin that's either glowing or breaking out, and the early chapters of heartburn for some.

And the appetite question everyone asks: no, you are not actually "eating for two" — most guidelines suggest only a few hundred extra calories a day in the second trimester, like an extra nourishing snack. Quality beats quantity: protein, iron-rich foods, calcium, and color on the plate. If your brain feels pleasantly foggy some days — the famous "pregnancy brain" — you're in enormous company; sleep, lists, and grace for yourself are the official prescription.

Quickening: the first flutters

Now, the moment everyone asks about: when will you feel your baby move? For most women, those first movements — called "quickening" — arrive somewhere between about sixteen and twenty-five weeks. If this is your first baby, it often lands later in that window, around twenty weeks or beyond; if you've been pregnant before, you may recognize them earlier, because you know what you're listening for.

And "listening" is the right word, because the first flutters don't feel like kicks at all. Women describe them as bubbles popping, a butterfly brushing past, popcorn, or a tiny fish flicking its tail — they're so subtle that most first-time moms realize only in hindsight, "oh, THAT was the baby." A few things affect timing: if your placenta is on the front wall of the uterus, it cushions the movements like a pillow and you may feel them later; most women notice movements most when lying still and quiet, often in the evening. So if you're sixteen weeks and feel nothing: that is completely expected, and it says nothing about how your baby is doing. One sweet note for partners: movements felt from the outside usually come a few weeks after mom feels them from within — not a snub, just the schedule.

Looking ahead: the week-20 anatomy scan

While we're looking ahead, preview your next big appointment: the mid-pregnancy anatomy scan, usually scheduled between about eighteen and twenty-two weeks — most often around week twenty. This is the detailed ultrasound where the sonographer measures your baby's head, heart, spine, stomach, kidneys, and limbs, checks the placenta and the fluid, and confirms how growth is tracking. It's the longest, most thorough look of the pregnancy.

Also on the horizon, just so nothing surprises you: a screening test for gestational diabetes is typically done a bit later, around twenty-four to twenty-eight weeks. Nothing to do about that now — just file it under "future me."

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially if heavy or with cramping · severe or one-sided pelvic pain — remember, round ligament pain is brief and positional, so pain that is strong, constant, or one-sided and doesn't ease is a different story · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine or dizziness · fainting · fluid leaking from the vagina. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 16

  • Book the anatomy scan if you haven't — the window is weeks 18–22, most often around week 20
  • Think extra nourishing snack, not a second dinner — protein, iron-rich foods, calcium, and color on the plate
  • Move gently when you stand, cough or roll over — round ligament twinges love sudden moves
  • Keep a list handy for foggy-brain days — sleep, lists, and grace for yourself
  • Lie still and quiet in the evening and just notice — first flutters often introduce themselves then

Key takeaways

  • Your avocado-sized baby can sense light, and hearing is wiring up next
  • Energy and appetite often return as hormone levels steady
  • First flutters arrive somewhere between weeks 16–25 — feeling nothing yet is normal
  • The anatomy scan (weeks 18–22) is your next big appointment — get it booked
