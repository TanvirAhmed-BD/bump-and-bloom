Pregnancy Week 18: Your Baby Can Hear!

This week, we need to talk about sound. Your bell pepper–sized baby's ears have settled into their final position, and the tiny hearing system inside is switching on — from here on, your baby lives in a world of muffled rhythm, and your voice is the headline act. For many families, this is also anatomy scan week. Here's your complete guide to week 18.

Your baby this week

Your baby is about the size of a bell pepper — roughly fourteen centimeters, about five and a half inches from head to bottom, and weighing around one hundred and ninety grams. The ears have moved into their final position on the sides of the head, and the bones of the inner ear, along with the nerve endings from the brain, are developed enough that hearing is beginning.

What does your baby hear? Not crisp sound — think of listening underwater. The soundtrack is your body: the steady drum of your heartbeat, blood moving through vessels, your stomach's entire symphony. Over the coming weeks, sounds from outside — voices, music, a dog's bark — will filter through, muffled and low. Your own voice travels best of all, carried through your body itself, which is why babies so often recognize and prefer their mother's voice after birth. It's never too early to talk, read, or sing to the bump; your partner's voice is learning its way in too.

Elsewhere this week: the nervous system is insulating itself — a fatty coating called myelin is forming around the nerves, speeding up the messages between them — and your baby yawns, hiccups, swallows, and cycles through sleep and waking. Taste buds are operational, sampling the amniotic fluid, whose flavors shift subtly with your meals. And a detail many parents find astonishing: if your baby is a girl, her own tiny uterus and fallopian tubes have already formed and taken their place — generations, nested like dolls.

Your body & symptoms: the official bump

At eighteen weeks, many women describe a turning point in the mirror: the bump is no longer "maybe" — it's a bump, announced and official, and maternity clothes graduate from optional to essential. Your uterus is approaching your belly button, and as it rises and tips your center of gravity forward, backache is a frequent companion. Gentle stretching, good chairs, and not sitting or standing in one position too long are the everyday tools.

The dizziness deserves its headline now, because week eighteen is prime time for it: blood vessels relaxed, blood volume high, and a uterus that can press on major vessels when you lie flat or stand suddenly. The classics still work — rise in stages, hydrate, eat small and often, and if a wave hits while you're standing, sit or lean until it passes. A new arrival for some women: swelling in the feet and ankles by evening, especially after long days or warm weather — feet up, a walk to get the circulation moving, and comfortable shoes usually handle it. Your appetite remains a loyal friend, heartburn may be moving in, and sleep is still mostly good — though finding a comfortable position becomes a nightly little project now. Pillow engineering: commence.

Quickening: are those flutters the baby?

Quickening — those first felt movements — arrives for most women somewhere between about sixteen and twenty-five weeks, and week eighteen sits right in the heart of the window. If this is your second or later pregnancy, you may already be feeling them — experienced moms recognize the signature early. If this is your first, the odds are good that your moment lands between now and about week twenty-two — and it may already have happened without your knowing, because the first movements are master disguisers: bubbles, popcorn, a butterfly's wing, a tiny fish flicking past.

A few honest variables: a placenta positioned on the front wall of the uterus acts like a cushion and mutes the show, sometimes by weeks; your build, your baby's position, and how still you are all matter — which is why most first flutters are noticed lying quietly in the evening. There is no test to pass this week, and nothing about your baby's health can be read from whether you've felt a tap yet. What's coming is worth the wait: within a few weeks, flutters graduate into unmistakable nudges.

The anatomy scan: what actually happens

For many of you, this week also holds the appointment circled since the first trimester: the anatomy scan, offered between about eighteen and twenty-two weeks. It's an external ultrasound like your earlier ones, but longer — often thirty to forty-five minutes — because the sonographer is taking a complete inventory: measurements of the head, abdomen, and thigh bone to track growth; a careful look at the heart's chambers, the spine, the stomach, the kidneys, the bladder, the brain's structures, the face, and all those fingers and toes; plus the placenta's position, the cord, and the fluid around your baby.

Two things to know going in. First, babies have schedules of their own: if yours is curled away from the camera, you may be sent for a walk, a cold drink, or even a second appointment on another day to finish the remaining views. That's about angles, not alarms — needing more pictures is common and usually purely technical. Second, this scan can share a lot of information; most families leave reassured, with a strip of photos. Sometimes the sonographer will say a doctor needs to review something, or recommend a follow-up look in a few weeks — and while that wait is genuinely hard, many follow-ups end in "all normal" or "just needed a better view." Your doctor will walk through anything specific with you; let them be your interpreter rather than the internet at 2 a.m. And if you want to know the sex, this is usually the moment — just tell the sonographer your preference at the start, including if you don't want to know, so nothing slips.

⚠️ Warning signs — call your doctor the same day

Vaginal bleeding, especially heavy or with cramping · severe or one-sided pelvic pain that doesn't ease · a fever of 100.4°F (38°C) or higher · vomiting where you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · signs of dehydration, like very dark urine or dizziness · fainting (lightheaded waves are common, but actually passing out always gets a call) · fluid leaking from the vagina. And on swelling: mild evening swelling in feet and ankles is typical — but sudden swelling of your face or hands, or one leg that's notably more swollen, red, or painful than the other, is not typical; that's a same-day call too. If something feels wrong, making the call is always the right move.

✅ Mama checklist — Week 18

  • Book or confirm your anatomy scan (it's offered between weeks 18–22) — and tell the sonographer at the start whether you want to know the sex
  • Talk, read, or sing to the bump a little every day — your voice is the sound your baby hears best
  • Rise in stages, sip water through the day, and eat small and often to keep dizziness away
  • Feet up in the evening if your ankles swell, and choose comfortable shoes
  • Start your pillow arrangement for side-sleeping — bump supported, hips comfortable

Key takeaways

  • Hearing is switching on — and your voice is the sound your baby hears best
  • Flutters can arrive any time in the 16–25 week window; none yet at 18 weeks is normal
  • The anatomy scan is a 30–45 minute head-to-toe inventory; callbacks for more views are usually about angles
  • Swelling red flags: sudden face or hand swelling, or one leg much more swollen, red, or painful
