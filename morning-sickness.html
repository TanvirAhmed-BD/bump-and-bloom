Morning Sickness: 12 Remedies That Actually Help

If you're reading this with one hand on a packet of crackers, feeling seasick on dry land — welcome, you're in the right place, and you are absolutely not alone. Around seven in ten pregnant women experience nausea, and despite its name, "morning sickness" can show up at noon, at midnight, or simply move in and refuse to leave. Here are twelve remedies that genuinely help most women — plus the important signs that what you're feeling is more than morning sickness and deserves medical help. Let's get you feeling more human.

Why it happens (and why it's not your fault)

Morning sickness is driven mainly by the rapid rise of pregnancy hormones — especially hCG and estrogen — combined with a more sensitive stomach and a superhuman sense of smell. It typically builds in the first weeks, peaks around weeks nine to eleven, and eases by about weeks twelve to fourteen for most women. Some feel it longer, and a smaller number feel it for much of the pregnancy.

Three things worth knowing. It is not caused by anything you ate, did, or failed to do. Your baby is remarkably well protected in there — even if your diet is temporarily beige and boring, your body prioritizes the baby, drawing on your reserves to keep building. And the goal of remedies is not a cure — nothing legitimately promises that — but turning the volume down from a nine to a four, so you can function, eat something, and get through the day. Try them like experiments: keep what helps you, drop what doesn't. Also know that nausea has favorite accomplices — heat, stuffy rooms, long car rides, being overtired, being ravenous. Several remedies below are really about disarming those accomplices, which is why they work better in combination than alone.

Remedies 1–3: eating foundations

Remedy one: eat small, and eat often. An empty stomach is nausea's best friend — acid with nothing to do makes everything worse. Instead of three big meals, graze: six small ones, a handful of something every couple of hours. Pair a carbohydrate with a little protein when you can — crackers with cheese, toast with peanut butter, yogurt with granola — because the combination keeps blood sugar steadier, and steady blood sugar means steadier stomachs.

Remedy two: the bedside cracker trick. Keep plain crackers, dry cereal, or a rice cake on your nightstand. Eat a little before you even sit up, then wait fifteen or twenty minutes before rising — slowly. Mornings are the worst because your stomach has been empty for eight hours. This humble trick is the one women have sworn by for generations, because it works on the actual problem.

Remedy three: ginger, in whatever form you'll accept it. Ginger tea, ginger chews, ginger ale made with real ginger, ginger capsules, even ginger biscuits. Ginger has been studied for pregnancy nausea more than almost any natural remedy, and many providers are perfectly comfortable with it in ordinary food-and-tea amounts. As with anything beyond food, check with your own provider about what's right for you — but as a first line, warm ginger tea is a very good place to start.

Remedies 4–6: fluids & trusted helpers

Remedy four: rethink how you drink. Big glasses of water on an empty or queasy stomach often come straight back up. Instead: small sips, constantly, throughout the day. Many women find very cold drinks easier — ice water, ice chips, frozen fruit bars, lemonade. Try drinking mostly between meals rather than with them, so your stomach isn't sloshing. If plain water tastes awful — a very common complaint — add lemon, cucumber, or a splash of juice, or try sparkling water; the bubbles genuinely help some women. Broths and electrolyte drinks count too. Your goal isn't a number of glasses; it's urine that stays pale yellow.

Remedy five: vitamin B6. This is the remedy to discuss with your provider, because it's one of the first things many of them suggest for pregnancy nausea — a simple, inexpensive vitamin with a long track record of use in pregnancy. Your provider can tell you whether it's appropriate for you and what amount makes sense. The key message is: don't just suffer assuming nothing safe exists — ask. There is a ladder of help, and B6 is often the first rung.

Remedy six: acupressure wrist bands. These elastic bands press on a point on the inner wrist — the P6 or Nei-Guan point — borrowed from acupuncture tradition. The scientific evidence is mixed, honestly, but the bands are inexpensive, completely drug-free, and a loyal army of women swears by them for cars, commutes, and queasy afternoons. If they help you, they help you — that's enough.

Remedies 7–9: senses & surroundings

Remedy seven: go cold, and go sour. Hot food throws off far more smell — and smell is the trigger for most nausea. Cold and room-temperature foods are much easier to face: sandwiches, salads, yogurt, fruit, cheese and crackers. And sour or tart flavors — lemonade, citrus segments, pickles, sour candies — cut through nausea surprisingly well for many women. There's a reason the stereotype of the pregnant woman and the pickles exists.

Remedy eight: fresh air, and trigger detective work. Open a window before you cook. Step outside for two minutes when a wave hits — cool air on the face genuinely settles many stomachs. And keep a tiny note on your phone for a few days: when nausea spikes, what just happened? Cooking smells, an empty stomach, the car, a particular perfume, brushing your teeth? Within days you'll see YOUR pattern, and a pattern you can see is a pattern you can dodge. If cooking smells are your nemesis, cold meals, a slow cooker on the porch, or a partner on cooking duty are all legitimate strategies.

Remedy nine: rest — strategically and without guilt. Fatigue is a nausea multiplier; almost every woman notices she's sickest when she's most tired. A short nap, an earlier bedtime, sitting down before you're desperate rather than after — these aren't indulgences, they're treatment. If you work, even ten horizontal minutes at lunch can reset your stomach for the afternoon. Two more from the senses department, because they cost nothing: something cold pressed to the neck or wrists — a chilled cloth, a cold drink can — calms many women mid-wave; and a mint or lemon sweet kept in a pocket gives your taste buds a clean reboot. Small sensory resets, outsized relief.

Remedies 10–12: smart routines

Remedy ten: reschedule your prenatal vitamin. This one surprises people: that big, iron-rich tablet on an empty morning stomach is a nausea bomb for many women. Try taking it with food, or in the evening before bed, when your stomach is settled and you'll sleep through any queasiness. If it's still a problem, tell your provider — there are different formulations, and they may adjust the plan with you. Don't just quietly stop it without mentioning it; a two-minute conversation fixes this one.

Remedy eleven: time your toothbrush. Brushing teeth is a top-five gag trigger in pregnancy. Two adjustments: rinse your mouth with water or a mild mouthwash first thing instead of brushing on a raw, empty stomach, and after being sick or eating, wait about thirty minutes before brushing — stomach acid softens enamel, and brushing immediately scrubs it. When you do brush, a smaller brush head and a flavor you can tolerate make a real difference.

Remedy twelve: build your nausea kit, and sniff strategically. Keep a small kit where life happens — handbag, car, desk: crackers, ginger chews, sour sweets, a bottle of water, a spare top if you're a real optimist. And for sudden smells — the bus, the office fridge — a handkerchief or sleeve with a drop of lemon or peppermint scent, or simply a fresh lemon to scratch and sniff, can be a genuine rescue. Citrus scent is the fastest legal stomach-settler most women own. Small preparations, big dignity. You deserve both.

Quick answers: questions everyone asks

"Does morning sickness mean a healthier pregnancy?" Some studies have found an association between nausea and a lower chance of miscarriage — but it's an association, not a requirement. Plenty of women with zero nausea have textbook pregnancies. If you're sick, take it as a rough sign hormones are active; if you're not, count yourself lucky, not worried.

"Is it okay that all I can eat is crackers and toast right now?" For a stretch of weeks — yes. Your baby takes what it needs from you, and this is exactly what prenatal vitamins are for. Mention your diet at your appointments, expand it as your stomach allows, and don't let anyone guilt you about the beige era. It ends.

"How fast do remedies work?" Think days, not minutes. Ginger, B6, and the eating patterns usually show their effect over two or three days of consistency. Give each experiment a fair trial of a few days before judging it — unless it's on the warning list below, in which case, the phone, immediately.

⚠️ When it's more than morning sickness — call your provider

For some women, nausea crosses into a condition called hyperemesis gravidarum, and that needs medical care, not more crackers. Call your provider promptly if: you can't keep fluids down for about twenty-four hours · you're losing weight noticeably · you're urinating very little, or your urine is very dark · you feel dizzy, faint, or your heart races when you stand · or you simply cannot function day to day. There is real help for this — IV fluids, prescription anti-nausea medicines your provider considers appropriate in pregnancy, and closer monitoring. Needing them is not a failure. If you're wondering whether it's bad enough to call — it is. Make the call.

✅ Your 12 remedies — the checklist

  • Eat small and often — never let your stomach sit empty
  • Crackers by the bed: eat before you rise, then wait 15–20 minutes
  • Ginger — tea, chews, real ginger ale, biscuits
  • Sip small and cold all day; save big drinks for between meals
  • Vitamin B6 — ask your provider if it's right for you
  • Acupressure wrist bands for cars, commutes and queasy afternoons
  • Cold foods and sour flavors — lemonade, citrus, pickles
  • Fresh air + a trigger diary on your phone for a few days
  • Real rest — naps and earlier nights are treatment, not indulgence
  • Move your prenatal vitamin to with food, or to bedtime
  • Brush later, gently — rinse first, wait ~30 minutes after eating
  • A nausea kit in bag, car and desk — plus a lemon to sniff

Key takeaways

  • Around 7 in 10 moms get morning sickness; it usually peaks at weeks 9–11 and eases by weeks 12–14
  • An empty stomach is nausea's best friend — grazing, ginger and smart sipping turn the volume down
  • You don't need all twelve remedies — you'll find your three or four, and they'll carry you
  • Can't keep fluids down for ~24 hours, dark urine, dizziness or weight loss = call your provider promptly
